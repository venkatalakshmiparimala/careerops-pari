**"Walk me through an analysis you led from data to recommendation."**

Use the **Entrata 1.6M Records AI Pipeline** story:
> "At Entrata, I led a project to automate income limit data processing across 1.6 million HUD and Tax Credit records. The prior process was manual Excel operations — error-prone and not auditable. The result was 100% production output with zero compliance incidents across all client environments. The key decision I made was treating output validation as non-negotiable — on regulated financial data, silent failures (wrong outputs that don't error) are more dangerous than loud ones."

**"Explain a complex risk concept using an analogy to a non-technical business stakeholder."**

Prepare one example of translating technical risk language for non-technical stakeholders. Suggested answer:

That framing — concrete, single-variable, outcome-focused — worked consistently with operations teams who didn't need to understand the underlying HUD formula."

**"Tell me about a time you worked on a team project with disagreement or conflict."**

Use the **300+ Code Reviews / Mentorship** story, reframed:
> "At Entrata I led code reviews across a team where senior engineers sometimes disagreed on approach — specifically around database schema decisions for a new compliance module. One engineer wanted a denormalized structure for query speed; I advocated for normalization for auditability, since we were dealing with regulated data. I ran a benchmarking exercise showing that the query speed difference was negligible at our data volume while the auditability gain was significant for compliance audits. The team agreed to go normalized. The key was making it an evidence conversation, not a preference conversation."

> "I led the microservices architecture redesign at Entrata — converting 20% of product modules into Docker containerized services. I wasn't the manager; I was the technical lead who had to align 5 engineers, get buy-in from the product team on the migration timeline, and ensure zero downtime for live client environments. The result was 40% faster deployments and 50% better scalability. The leadership challenge was managing the uncertainty during migration — I held daily syncs during rollout and kept a real-time status board so no one was operating blind."

I spent 8 years implementing the exact compliance frameworks Fannie Mae's multifamily programs define: LIHTC, HCV, HUD, AMI-based pricing, income recertification. - **Do NOT volunteer:** Specific comp number before they share the band — defer with the script above

- **Red flags they screen for:** OPT without H-1B sponsor plan = immediate risk; comp mismatch above band; vague motivation ("I want to learn finance")

### To the hiring manager

- **Lead with:** The domain overlap — 8 years Entrata = 8 years inside the data that Fannie Mae's multifamily risk team analyzes. "I've automated the workflows your programs define"
- **Vocabulary to use:** "loss mitigation," "credit risk transfer," "loan performance," "underwriting standards," "AMI thresholds," "income eligibility determination," "portfolio monitoring" — these are Fannie Mae's words, not generic finance words
- **Sharp questions (pick 2):**
  - "Which of the three pillars of Fannie Mae's MF credit risk framework — performance management, mitigation, or credit risk transfer — does this role spend the most time supporting?"
  - "What does the data infrastructure look like for risk analysis — direct SQL on loan data, or is there a more structured data warehouse layer?"
  - "What's changed in the multifamily portfolio risk profile in the last 12–18 months that's shifted how the team prioritizes its work?"
- **What NOT to do:** Don't frame your background as "software engineering transitioning to finance" — frame it as "domain expert with technical skills coming to the institution that defines the policy I was implementing"

### To the peer/technical panel

- **Lead with:** Proof points that show analytical rigor AND domain knowledge together — the 1.6M HUD records pipeline; the Isolation Forest anomaly detection at GMU; the AMI eligibility verification automation
- **Things to avoid:** The one reported Glassdoor concern for this level is being too abstract. The peer Lead Associate will respect specifics — "I wrote this SQL," "I used Isolation Forest," "I ran a benchmark" — over vague statements about "having analytical experience"
- **Reverse questions for the peer Lead Associate:**
  - "What does a week actually look like for a Lead Associate on this team — is it more data wrangling and pipeline work, or more interpretation and stakeholder reporting?"
  - "What surprised you most when you joined vs. what you expected the role to be?"
  - "What's the hardest analysis problem this team is working on right now?"
