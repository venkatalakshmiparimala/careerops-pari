## H) Draft Application Answers

**Why CLEAR?**
> CLEAR's mission — making experiences safer and easier — maps directly to the AI work I find most meaningful: building systems that are reliable and trustworthy, not just capable. At Intuit and in LeaseI, I've built AI with guardrails, evals, and HITL enforced in code — not as an afterthought, but as the architecture. I want to bring that same approach to CLEAR's 43M+ member platform, where the stakes of a wrong AI decision are real.

**Describe your experience building AI systems for production:**
> At Intuit, I own the full loop for Anthropic Claude-powered features: design structured-output schemas, build RAG grounding pipelines, write LLM-as-judge regression tests in CI, and add guardrails at the code layer (input validation, sensitive-data redaction, output schema enforcement, fallback logic). Before that, I built LeaseI — an 8-agent LangGraph platform for regulated compliance workflows with HITL escalation when confidence is low and a safety boundary adversarially tested to never auto-resolve eligibility decisions for a tenant. Both shipped with eval harnesses, not just unit tests.

**Describe a time you had to balance AI capability with safety/responsibility:**
> The core design rule in LeaseI (enforced in code everywhere): the LLM extracts and reasons; a human or deterministic validator makes every final eligibility, compliance, and financial call. Not a guideline — a hard architectural constraint. I tested the tenant self-service agent adversarially to make sure it never auto-resolved a regulated decision even at high confidence. The answer to "can you make this work?" was always "yes — but only if the guardrail holds first."
